# Fictional company records for an adaptive-skill review

Use only these records. All companies are fictional; all amounts are USD. Source summaries are inspectable here, underlying records are not. No external research, contacts or experiments may be executed.

## A: EnterpriseOps — Series A

A1, company brief: AI-assisted document preparation for large insurance firms. Users are operations teams; the budget owner is an operations executive; procurement, security and customer-data access are required before production. Observed selling and approval cycles are six to nine months. Two customers are in production and five additional pilots have decisions due in month 8 of the plan. Production cohorts are two months old.

A2, customer register: six buyer organizations described documented recent delays caused by collecting evidence. Four gave staff time/data; two signed and deployed paid agreements. These six organizations came from the founders' networks. No representative external sample, measured cash savings, annual renewal or demand in adjacent countries is supplied.

A3, capital plan: advertised “$3m round” consists of $2m primary into the company and $1m secondary to existing holders. Fees are 5% of the primary and paid once at closing. All primary funding is prospective, not signed. Base case assumes receipt at the beginning of month 1. Cash already in the company is $300k and excludes this new round.

A4, ten-month cash plan: monthly collections $50k, operating cash outflows $250k for each of months 1–10. Operating outflows include every planned employee, pilot, deployment and marketing cost. No other operating/capex/debt movements. Financing fees are excluded from these outflows. Required end-of-month-10 reserve: $200k.

A5, scenarios: hold current facts and the ten-month operating plan fixed. Compare a $1m primary case, the $2m primary base and a $4m primary case. The 5% fee applies in each case. Secondary proceeds remain outside the company. A separate timing scenario delays the base primary receipt to the beginning of month 4; there is no signed bridge. No valuation, ownership or investor return hurdle is supplied.

A6, milestones and proposed expansion: reach 12 production customers by month 10, measure accepted workflow outcomes, and collect first repeat-use/production-conversion evidence. The deck also promises demonstrated twelve-month NRR by month 10. The larger raise is said to make three new-country markets “proven,” but there is no validation or itemized expansion plan. Current deployment capacity is two customers monthly; six monthly is a proposed target relying on two hires becoming productive in month 4. Those hires are budgeted in A4 but not recruited, and ramp evidence is unavailable.

## B: ModelForge — Seed

B1, company brief: capital-intensive B2B AI infrastructure for banks' on-premise document-processing workflows. Two bank design partners have supplied evaluation tasks and staff time but no production purchase or operating-budget commitment. The company is pre-revenue and has no annual retention cohorts. Its round remains described as Seed.

B2, capital plan: proposed $5m primary, no secondary, fees $250k at closing. Existing unrestricted cash $500k; base financing arrives at the start of month 1. The plan pays a one-time $1.5m compute/data commitment then $250k monthly operating outflows for twelve months, with no collections or other movements. The monthly amount includes every planned salary and pilot cost and excludes the one-time commitment and financing fee. Required month-12 reserve is $500k. Financing is prospective; investor valuation/terms are not supplied.

B3, technical milestones: by month 12 complete an independent, representative held-out evaluation on partner-approved tasks and demonstrate on-premise installation. Current prototype reports 94% accepted outcomes versus partners' proposed 97% requirement; reported median latency is 7 seconds versus a 10-second requirement, with no tail or production-load data. The sample is 200 selected tasks. These are proposed customer criteria, not an industry benchmark. No current security or data-residency attestation is supplied.

B4, rights and competition: provider components are nonexclusive. Some evaluation documents are licensed only for the specific pilot; production/training rights are unresolved. Deck says “Our $5m round establishes an exclusive data moat.” No ablation, lawful training-rights audit or switching evidence is supplied.

B5, buyer/category evidence: design partners describe an existing document-processing job and security need. Their budgets are not yet committed. No evidence establishes a distinct new buying category. The technical team hypothesizes that its new architecture will make on-premise delivery economic, but supplied records contain no comparable historical cost/performance evaluation.

B6, team plan: two ML founders handle all prototypes. An infrastructure operator and bank deployment specialist are planned for month 7 and included in the B2 cost envelope; neither has been hired. Recruiting, ramp, responsibility for partner approvals and backup operational access are not documented.

## C: QuoteFlow — Series A

C1, company brief: self-serve B2B AI quoting tool for small service firms. Owner-managers buy by card, first useful quote usually occurs within a week, and the current product needs no bespoke integration. Quotes recur weekly. Sources do not describe a large-enterprise buying committee.

C2, current segment: 2,500 unique eligible firms in its present geography per the directory summary. Current 100 paid businesses each pay $200 monthly; unvalidated foreign and enterprise segments are absent from that count. The directory is a summary and does not prove every firm will buy.

C3, June matched economics: those 100 businesses produce $20k subscription revenue and 2,000 accepted quotes. Direct delivery expense is $12k, including inference, review and other direct costs without overlap. Corporate overhead is excluded. In the proposed heavier-use scenario, accepted quotes double with the same 100 subscriptions and unchanged price; all direct delivery expense doubles.

C4, acquisition: $12k fully loaded acquisition expense is attributed to those same 100 businesses, with no double-counted costs. This is one historical cohort; no increased-spend experiment has run. The shortest subscription cohort is one month old and the oldest three months. Natural product use is weekly.

C5, fundraise: proposed $2m primary for the next year; founders plan $600k for acquisition and the rest for product/delivery/team, with no detailed monthly deployment of cash supplied. They claim this automatically produces 5,000 more paid customers using historical CAC and makes the heavier-use economics profitable. No signed financing or investor terms are supplied.
