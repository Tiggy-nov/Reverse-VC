# Annotated findings from fictional cases

These are edited illustrations drawn from the synthetic evaluations, not verbatim provider transcripts or actual investment recommendations. All business inputs come from [company-cases.md](company-cases.md); locators below refer to its numbered records. Underlying contracts, CRM records and workbook formulas were unavailable.

## EnterpriseOps: the round buys a proof window

**Applied lens:** Series A enterprise workflow software with six-to-nine-month approvals, two production customers and five pending pilots (A1).

The advertised $3m round contains $2m primary and $1m secondary. Only the primary enters the company. After its 5% fee, net primary is $1.9m. The plan burns $200k net monthly: $250k outflows less $50k collections. All planned operating costs are already included (A3–A4).

| Primary case | Existing cash + net primary | Cash after month 10 | Interpretation |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| $1m | $1.25m | −$750k | First negative month-end is month 7; the plan needs intervention before then |
| $2m | $2.2m | $200k | Exactly the required terminal reserve, conditional on timely closing |
| $4m | $4.1m | $2.1m | Additional liquidity under the same operating plan |

If the base funding arrives at the beginning of month 4, month-end cash is $100k, −$100k and −$300k before closing. The later financing does not eliminate the earlier cash breach. At least $300k temporary liquidity is needed through month 3 on these monthly totals; intramonth timing and bridge terms remain unknown (A5).

**GTM consequence:** the smaller case cannot reach the month-8 pilot decisions under the fixed plan. The base case can fund that window, but five pilots alone cannot supply the ten additional customers needed to reach twelve production customers. More cash does not shorten procurement or prove new-country demand (A1, A6).

**Evidence to obtain:** a dated buyer/approval/conversion schedule, additional qualified accounts, and evidence that planned hires can reach the assumed deployment capacity. The oldest customers may reach twelve-month observation by month 10 if exact dates align; new conversions cannot. Do not automatically reject the chronology or claim broad annual retention from two customers.

## ModelForge: a $5m round can still be Seed

**Applied lens:** pre-revenue, capital-intensive AI infrastructure for bank on-premise workflows. Two design partners provided tasks and staff time, not production budgets (B1).

The supplied plan ends month 12 with $750k:

```text
$500k existing cash
+$5m primary
−$250k closing fees
−$1.5m compute/data commitment
−12 × $250k operating outflows
=$750k
```

That is $250k above the required reserve, conditional on financing arriving as planned. Salaries and pilots are already in the operating envelope (B2).

**Technical consequence:** capital can fund a representative held-out evaluation and an on-premise installation milestone. It does not establish those outcomes today. The selected prototype tasks show 94% acceptance versus a proposed 97% partner criterion; production-load and latency-tail evidence are missing (B3).

**Defensibility consequence:** prospective funding creates no exclusivity. Pilot-only data rights and nonexclusive provider components leave the claimed data moat unsupported (B4). Seed evaluation should examine the next technical and commercial proof, rather than demand annual SaaS retention from a pre-revenue company.

## QuoteFlow: acquisition spend cannot override market arithmetic

**Applied lens:** self-serve B2B software for small firms. Owner-managers pay by card and use quotes weekly; the case does not support imposing an enterprise buying committee (C1).

The current eligibility ceiling is 2,500 firms × $200/month × 12 = **$6m annual revenue at full penetration and unchanged price**. That is conditional capacity, not demonstrated demand. With 100 current customers, only 2,400 additional firms remain in that count (C2).

Historical acquisition cost is $12k ÷ 100 = $120 per customer. Dividing the proposed $600k acquisition budget by that historical CAC produces 5,000 mechanically; it does not establish marginal CAC or supply a larger eligible market (C4–C5).

June direct delivery economics are $20k revenue − $12k delivery expense = $8k, or 40%, before corporate overhead. Doubled usage at unchanged subscriptions and doubled delivery cost produces $20k − $24k = **−$4k, or −20%**. Raising $2m does not change this equation (C3, C5).

**Next test:** measure week-one activation, weekly retained value, matched delivery costs and incremental paid acquisition at bounded higher spend. Pricing or delivery-cost improvements need separate evidence. The missing monthly cash plan prevents a firm conclusion about the affordable experiment budget.

## What the examples demonstrate

Market, stage and funding change the relevant tests and the feasible evidence window. Current evidence, future plans and investor fit remain separate. These fictional cases do not establish the suite's accuracy on every company or constitute a live Claude execution test.
