# Business context and investor calibration

Read at intake and when the business model, buyer/market, proposed raise or target investor changes. Default to AI-native B2B/enterprise at Seed or Series A. Use [company-calibration.md](company-calibration.md) and [raise-calibration.md](raise-calibration.md) for the common adaptive method. Fill only what sources support; a missing field is a question, not a license to invent. Use one company per context brief.

## Minimum useful context

| Field | What changes the review |
| --- | --- |
| Product and job | Workflow before/after, user, economic buyer, budget owner, buying trigger, consequence of failure |
| Value and adoption | Measured outcome and baseline, time to value, pilot-to-paid conversion, production usage, customer evidence |
| Monetization | Seat, subscription, usage, outcome, licensing, services, transaction fee, or blend; who bears usage and failure costs |
| Revenue quality | Actual period revenue, recurring contract definition, annualization method, commitments, credits, services share, concentration |
| AI dependency | Owned vs third-party models, orchestration, retrieval, proprietary data rights, human intervention, provider dependence |
| Scale today | Product stage, customer and cohort ages, deployed capacity, geography, channel, integration and support effort |
| Scale next | Binding constraint, capacity/hiring plan, quality/cost tradeoff, next proof point, required capital and time |
| Fundraise | Current primary/secondary amount and currency, minimum/target/expanded cases, secured versus prospective funds, close timing, available cash, fees/obligations, uses and proof milestones; terms where relevant |
| Investor fit | Mandate, stage, check size, ownership, sector, geography, portfolio conflict if disclosed, return expectations |
| Basis | Source IDs, reporting dates, currency/FX, actual/forecast cutoff, definition changes, gaps |

If enough context exists, begin without an intake interview. When it does not, prioritize the missing business model, fundraise stage/ask, investor audience, and available materials. Request supporting files through the user's normal file-sharing mechanism; do not imply they have already been read.

## Route by economic model

Models may overlap. Select the relevant tests and explain exclusions.

| Archetype | Core scaling question | Evidence to prioritize |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Enterprise or vertical AI application | Can each additional deployment generate durable value without equivalent custom labor? | Production cohorts, deployment hours, buyer ROI, renewal quality, integrations, domain error costs |
| Outcome-priced agent or AI-enabled service | Does a successfully delivered outcome remain profitable after attempts, remediation, and human work? | Accepted outcomes, retries, dispute/refund rates, variable labor, cost distribution, outcome attribution |
| Usage-based developer tool or infrastructure | Does sustained workload growth create profitable usage and durable adoption? | Paid usage cohorts, reserved vs on-demand capacity, cloud/provider exposure, concentration, net revenue treatment |
| SMB/midmarket self-serve AI | Can qualified business users become retained paying accounts at viable cost? | User versus account identity, activation, buyer/payment authority, natural use cadence, channel cohorts, onboarding/support effort and usage-cost tails |
| Foundation model or capital-intensive infrastructure | Can differentiated performance and distribution repay compute and capital commitments? | Reproducible evaluations, utilization, capacity contracts, training/inference allocation, financing needs, distribution |
| Marketplace or transaction business with AI | Is growth profitable after incentives and execution costs, with durable liquidity? | GMV vs net revenue, take rate, repeat activity, subsidies, matching/fulfillment cost, concentration |

## Adjust the standard of proof

Seed and Series A evidence depends on the business and its actual maturity. A young company may have a promising hypothesis with limited histories; label it accordingly rather than fabricate mature cohorts. If the user explicitly requests growth-stage work, assess durable repeatability, reconciled revenue, selling/deployment capacity and the financing plan at that maturity.

For Seed, focus on problem evidence, differentiated insight, early pull or technical proof, expansion logic and the next capital-funded experiment. For Series A, seek increasingly repeatable customer or sector-appropriate milestone evidence, with cohort maturity and the acquisition/delivery motion understood. Use [early-stage-gates.md](early-stage-gates.md) to separate venture potential, readiness for the current round and fund-specific economics; no fixed ARR amount determines all three.

Use comparable cohorts and dates. A 2025 group of selected breakout businesses, a public-company median, and a 2026 Series B vertical AI company are not interchangeable comparables. Record why a benchmark fits before applying it; if it does not fit, use the company's own trend, contract economics, and operational constraints.

## Conviction chain

For each link, capture a claim, a source, contrary evidence, and the next test:

1. A specific buyer has an urgent, valuable problem.
2. The product produces a measurable outcome better than a realistic alternative.
3. Buyers will pay and continue using it under ordinary commercial terms.
4. The company can deliver that outcome at attractive economics for its model.
5. Acquisition, deployment, and support can repeat beyond founders and early adopters.
6. Advantages persist as models improve and competitors bundle similar functions.
7. The reachable market and expansion path can support the investor's return requirements.
8. The proposed capital buys milestones that make those claims substantially more certain.

Do not promote one link's evidence into another: a benchmark win is not customer ROI, paid pilots are not renewals, workflow access is not a durable moat, and a large budget category is not obtainable revenue.

For a fundraising review, show the current-raise-to-milestone bridge and the effect of a smaller, larger or delayed raise when decision-useful. Use company-specific costs and timing; neither a standard round-size bracket nor a fixed runway target substitutes for that plan.
