# Competition and defensibility

Question: why will buyers choose the company today, and what makes that position durable? These are related but different claims. Use [the shared test contract](../challenge-loop.md).

Inputs: buyer alternatives, win/loss records, current primary-source competitor information, switching/retention evidence, distribution access, data contracts, learning curves and the mechanism claimed to improve with scale.

## High-value tests

1. **Buyer-realistic alternatives.** Include incumbents, model providers, internal builds, manual work and doing nothing, alongside direct startups. Ask what the two most consequential alternatives do better and why customers still choose this product. Rank by observed buyer behavior rather than feature count. If no win/loss records exist, specify customer questions and label competitive positioning unverified.
2. **Mechanism of advantage.** Trace each claim to a causal chain: exclusive access or accumulated resource → measurable outcome → willingness to pay/retention/distribution benefit. For network effects, distinguish user-to-user value from economies of scale. For data, inspect rights, acquisition cost, reproducibility and incremental benefit. A large dataset alone is not evidence of a moat.
3. **Replication and bundling scenario.** Pick a relevant competitor and plausible resources/time horizon from context. Ask what it could copy, distribute or subsidize and what would then happen to differentiation, price and retention. Keep the funding/time shock illustrative rather than hardcoding the guide's figures. Compare replicable features with workflow, distribution, trust or integration barriers backed by evidence.
4. **Moat erosion or compounding.** Test a provider capability improvement, portability change or incumbent bundle. Identify observable evidence that the advantage is strengthening or weakening. Disconfirm a specific claimed barrier when records show customers switch cheaply or incremental data delivers no claimed benefit; do not conclude there is no advantage solely because another company makes a similar marketing claim.

Output: buyer-alternative matrix, competitive strengths and weaknesses, moat mechanism with evidence/gaps, and one coherent counter-move scenario. Narrow unsupported exclusivity claims and retain differentiation that the evidence supports.

Handoffs: technical novelty/rights → product; pricing power and retention → business model/finance; channel control → scalability; market/category boundaries → market and category/timing.
