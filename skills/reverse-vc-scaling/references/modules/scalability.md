# Scalability and execution capacity

Question: what breaks first when the company expands, and can the constraint be relieved economically? Use [the shared test contract](../challenge-loop.md).

Inputs: funnel conversion and cycle times, rep capacity/ramp, channel mix, cohort adoption, deployment backlog/hours, support and exception workloads, provider capacity, quality/load results, hiring/capex plans and cash model.

## High-value tests

1. **Demand-to-cash capacity chain.** Reconstruct leads → qualified opportunities → contracts → deployment → productive use → accepted billing → collection. Estimate capacity and delay at each stage from records. Identify the binding constraint and reconcile it to the revenue forecast. Signed demand above deployment capacity is not proof of near-term revenue scalability.
2. **Scale scenario with coupled assumptions.** Use the company's next milestone volume; choose illustrative multiples only when useful. Change workload mix, concurrent demand, onboarding, labor, failure/retry rates and cash timing together where the scenario implies it. An attractive average can conceal costly customer/usage tails. Distinguish a calculated scenario from an executed load test.
3. **Repeatability outside the founders.** Compare selling, onboarding and customer success with and without founder/custom-engineering intervention. Inspect cycle time, conversion, time to value and renewal by segment/channel. A growing services team may be a valid business choice, but its cost and capacity must appear in the business model and forecast.
4. **Constraint relief and resilience.** For each bottleneck, require a specific intervention, cost, owner, lead time and verification measure. Test supplier outage/rate limits, major customer concentration, hiring delay, or market expansion where material. A plan to hire or automate does not establish future productivity.

Output: a capacity chain with the bottleneck highlighted, a scenario tied to economics and cash, and a prioritized experiment or operating plan. Revise the growth and scale narrative to explain both mechanism and dependencies.

Handoffs: sales/education cost → business model/category; production quality → product; staffing/leadership → team; deployment dates, collections and costs → financial model; segment limits → market. Technical throughput, operational throughput and commercial repeatability require separate evidence.

Boundary: GTM strategy and channel selection are reviewed separately; reuse their demand/conversion assumptions and test capacity to execute them. A narrow throughput question does not require a new GTM audit.
