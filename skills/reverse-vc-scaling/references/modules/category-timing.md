# Category creation and why now

Question: is the company solving a distinct buying problem whose adoption can be evidenced, and why is this the right time? Category creation and timing are separate claims; test both when asserted. A company can be attractive within an existing category. Use [the shared test contract](../challenge-loop.md).

Inputs: customers' descriptions of the problem and alternatives, budget ownership, paid adoption and procurement evidence, messaging tests, dated changes in technical capability/cost, buyer behavior or applicable rules, sales cycles and education costs.

## High-value tests

1. **Category distinction from buyer behavior.** Ask whether customers independently describe a distinct job, assemble a different buying committee, use different success criteria, or move/create budget. Compare to the established category and current alternatives. A new label, analyst slide or founder vocabulary alone does not demonstrate a new market. Test the proposition with qualified buyers and observed purchasing behavior; label the category hypothesis unresolved when interviews or paid decisions are missing.
2. **Demand creation and capture.** Identify the initial wedge, adjacent spend, who must be educated, and who captures the resulting demand. Test whether the company bears the education cost while incumbents capture later purchases. Route education effort, longer cycles and new-budget friction into CAC, sales capacity and cash. Do not double-count new-category and displaced-category spending.
3. **Dated enabling change.** Name the change, primary evidence, causal mechanism and resulting buyer decision. Test a counterfactual: what was infeasible or uneconomic before, and what changed sufficiently? A series of documented gradual improvements can cross a relevant threshold; do not force a precise month or a dramatic regulatory event when the evidence shows a gradual transition. Separate general AI progress from company-specific advantage.
4. **Window, delay and reversal.** Test earlier/later adoption, a delayed rule or budget release, commodity capabilities, and competitor entry where material. Examine whether urgency is customer-driven and whether the business can survive the timing error. A forecast regulatory date is not guaranteed law or guaranteed demand; verify applicability from current official sources when used.

Output: category hypothesis/evidence table, existing-versus-new-category alternatives, timing mechanism/counterfactual, and a buyer validation plan with criteria and implications. Retain uncertainty in both market sizing and the investor story.

Handoffs: buyer universe/budget → market; substitutes/category leadership → competition; education cost/price capture → business model; adoption lag → scalability and cash. Do not count stronger naming as evidence of demand.
