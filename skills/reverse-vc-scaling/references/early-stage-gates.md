# Customer problem and venture-scale gates

Use for Seed/Series A work or when the user asks whether the business is venture-backable or solves a worthwhile problem. These are foundational assessments that feed the nine domain modules; do not force them into a narrow task that already has adequate context.

Apply the shared [company calibration](company-calibration.md) and, when relevant, [raise calibration](raise-calibration.md). The default company is AI-native B2B/enterprise. Hold its observed facts fixed when comparing stage or funding cases, and separate primary company capital from secondary proceeds and historical fundraising.

## Optional standalone companions

When available in the environment, use `customer-problem-stress-test` for customer situation, consequence, priority, current alternatives, buyer/budget and validation evidence. Use `venture-scale-assessment` for the wedge-to-large-outcome path, proof appropriate to Seed/Series A, capital milestones and fund economics. Read their SKILL.md through the environment's skill mechanism when applying them. Do not assume they are installed elsewhere or require them to use this master skill.

Both may be invoked independently. Reuse their source evidence and claim IDs rather than treating a prior model verdict as independent support. Preserve contrary evidence and note stale or incomplete source coverage.

## Self-contained fallback

1. Define the beneficiary, user, payer, problem or desired outcome, trigger, current alternative and observed consequence. Separate what customers say from what they do; investigate sampling, commitment and adoption friction. Absence of existing spend does not automatically negate latent demand, and positive feedback does not prove purchasing intent.
2. Calibrate the proof standard. At Seed, assess insight, early behavior or technical proof, a credible entry/expansion path and the next decisive milestone. At Series A, test repeatability and economics in a defined segment or sector-appropriate technical/commercial milestones. Respect immature cohorts, founder-led selling and long-development-cycle businesses; do not invent mature data or use a universal revenue bar.
3. Reconstruct problem → wedge → adoption → expansion → capturable revenue/economics → company value → investor proceeds. Use the existing market, business-model, scalability and financial modules for the details. A small wedge can lead to a large business only through a credible expansion mechanism.
4. Give separate conclusions about venture-scale potential, evidence for the current round, and investor mandate economics. Useful/profitable does not guarantee fund fit; inadequate current proof does not establish a permanent market ceiling. Compute returns from actual terms where supplied, without imposing a whole-fund return requirement by default.

## Shared handoff

`Company/version | stage and investor lens | customer segment/problem | sources/claim IDs | observed behavior and limitations | scale/expansion assumptions | round-readiness gaps | return basis or unknown | proposed tests/owners | affected modules`

Carry customer value and human-intervention findings to product/business model; buyer population and budgets to market; adoption friction to scalability; category uncertainty to timing; capital and ownership assumptions to finance. Deduplicate objections that share the same underlying evidence.

If the problem is weakly evidenced, identify the next discovery test while keeping any promising long-term opportunity conditional. If the problem is strong but the supported economic scope is narrow, explain the constraint and the expansion proof needed. Do not convert either conclusion into a formal investment decision or a guaranteed fundraising outcome.
