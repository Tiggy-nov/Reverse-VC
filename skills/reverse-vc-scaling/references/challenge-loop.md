# Evidence-grounded challenge loop

Use for business stress tests, adversarial rehearsal, or IC challenges. Carry the strongest supported positive thesis alongside the risks. Firm questions are useful; performative hostility and invented objections are not.

## Create a falsifiable test

For each decision-critical claim, record a compact test card:

`Test ID | claim/source | alternative explanation or failure mechanism | affected investor decision | evidence population/period | procedure and metric | acceptance/failure criterion and basis | actual result or not run | required proof/action | proposed owner | linked assumptions`

Choose the smallest test that can change a decision: a contract reconciliation, representative customer cohort, counterfactual market scenario, customer interview, quality evaluation, or capacity model. Distinguish analytical checks performed on supplied records from experiments requiring new execution. Preparing an eval design does not establish model quality; a proposed reference call is not a completed reference.

Criteria should come from customer commitments, observed economics, operating requirements, investor mandate, or explicitly justified assumptions. Where no defensible threshold exists, propose one for validation and keep the result unresolved. Do not adopt universal pass marks from a blog or declare failure merely because a source is missing.

Run the relevant tests in this order when useful: reconstruct the claim → seek disconfirming evidence → calculate/inspect → state the consequence → repair the material or specify the business action → retest the linked claim. Questions are tools for discovering evidence, not evidence themselves.

## Founder interrogation mode

1. Establish the company, stage, investor lens, requested domain, and evidence already available. Start with a question anchored to a consequential claim or omission.
2. Ask one answerable question, then wait. In live conversation, use the available user-input mechanism or end the turn with the question. Do not invent the founder's response or dump the entire question bank.
3. Evaluate the actual answer for specificity, source, denominator/time period, causal logic, and consistency with earlier answers. Identify exactly what remains unresolved. Treat hesitation or polish as a communication observation, not proof about the business.
4. Use a focused follow-up: request the missing record, test a counterexample, change a material assumption, or reconcile a conflicting answer. Usually one or two follow-ups are enough to determine that evidence is unavailable; log the gap and offer the next useful question instead of demanding an answer indefinitely.
5. Preserve the original answer and supporting evidence separately from suggested replacement wording. If the founder asks for help, supply a clearly labeled draft using known facts and explicit gaps, then let them respond.
6. At a category close, summarize the supported strengths, the material weaknesses, the one-line reason an investor could defer, and the evidence/action that would change that view. Do not invent two strengths or two weaknesses to fill a quota.

Retain context across challenges, with source IDs and timestamps. Carry unresolved questions forward, but update or retire objections when evidence resolves them. A claim repeated more confidently is still unverified. If a user asks for one module, do not force a full-company interview.

## Objection lifecycle

Use stable IDs and one of these states:

- `open — missing evidence`: a material question whose answer is unknown.
- `supported concern`: accessible evidence supports the failure mechanism.
- `resolved — evidence`: new or existing evidence addresses the stated concern.
- `resolved — clarification`: precise presentation fixes a reader misunderstanding; underlying business proof is unchanged.
- `accepted risk`: the concern remains and is disclosed with consequences and proposed mitigation.
- `invalid objection`: calculation, source reading, or investor premise was wrong; record the correction.
- `not applicable`: the objection does not fit the business model or mandate, with explanation.

Record what changed, the evidence, and what would reopen the issue. Do not erase inconvenient historical answers. A dropped claim resolves a presentation defect but does not prove the original commercial thesis.

## Cross-module consistency

Check at least the material dependencies:

- Product quality and human intervention ↔ cost per successful outcome ↔ pricing/margins.
- Reachable market and price ↔ penetration assumptions ↔ required exit value.
- Sales conversion ↔ deployment capacity ↔ revenue start ↔ collections/runway.
- Category education ↔ buying committee/budget ↔ CAC, sales-cycle length and capital need.
- Provider commoditization ↔ product differentiation ↔ price pressure ↔ retention.
- Hiring/leadership constraints ↔ delivery milestones ↔ payroll/burn and funding date.

Run coherent scenarios. For example, if the company needs additional customer-specific work to win a segment, change deployment effort and associated cost/cash timing as well as projected revenue. Do not test each workbook assumption in isolation when the scenario changes several together.

## IC challenge and adjudication

After available domain work, produce a focused bear memo with the few decision-driving reasons to defer or decline. For each: claim, supporting source, causal path to downside, uncertainty, and the evidence or action that could resolve it. Never invent enough reasons to fill a fixed count. Label it an adversarial exercise, not the committee's decision.

A bear-only rehearsal memo can omit the positive case from that particular section. It must still preserve uncertainty and contrary facts necessary to make its claims accurate. Then keep a separate adjudication: strongest supported positive thesis, valid concerns, invalid concerns, accepted risks, missing proof, and the simulated decision under the mandate.

Finish by testing the revised investor-facing package in a fresh neutral session where available, following [reviewer-prompts.md](reviewer-prompts.md). Keep the accumulated practice history out of that packet. If a fresh reviewer or Claude is unavailable, label the limitation and prepare the packet without claiming it ran.

Stop at the scoped evidence/readiness gates, the agreed budget, or the need for new business evidence. The objective is an answer that survives scrutiny, not a model that has run out of objections.
