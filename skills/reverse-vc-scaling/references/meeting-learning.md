# Investor meeting preparation and learning

Use when explicitly preparing for or debriefing a real meeting, not as mandatory extra work for every artifact review. Reuse the shared objection ledger and relevant modules; do not require another installed meeting skill.

## Before the meeting

Read available notes and current, attributable fund/partner information. Prefer dated official thesis/stage/geography information and the investor's public writing. Mark inferred interests and unverified portfolio conflicts separately. A historic investment does not establish a current mandate or predict a particular question with certainty.

Prepare a short brief: investor fit and gaps; the few most consequential likely questions with their source-based rationale; the strongest relevant company evidence; objections to address proactively; and unresolved facts to acknowledge. Preserve core facts across investors while adapting emphasis to their mandate.

For rehearsal, ask one investor-specific question at a time and evaluate the actual answer using [challenge-loop.md](challenge-loop.md). If fund information is sparse, use a generic lens and state the limitation rather than inventing preferences. Do not send the brief or contact the investor unless authorized.

## After the meeting

Use the supplied transcript/notes, or ask for recollections one question at a time. Prioritize actual questions/objections, the founder's answers, records requested, unexpected comparables or competitors, explicit next steps, and what needs to change before the next meeting. Preserve direct quotes only when supported; label paraphrases and recollections.

Separate:

- **Observed:** the investor asked for a retention cohort, questioned a cost allocation, or specified a next step.
- **Reported impression:** the founder perceived hesitation, enthusiasm or a change in tone.
- **Interpretation:** a possible concern, with alternative explanations and uncertainty.

Do not infer investment intent, hidden objections, or a funding probability from body language or politeness. If only a recollection is available, do not imply a transcript was reviewed. Timely notes help preserve detail, but a late debrief is still useful; no arbitrary time cutoff invalidates it.

## Update the system

Record `meeting/date | investor/mandate | observed question | answer given | evidence requested | linked objection/module | explicit next step | outcome when known`. Update or create an objection, route it to the relevant module, and identify the most consequential evidence or wording change.

Across meetings, report repeated themes with counts and denominator, distinguishing separate investors from repeated conversations with one fund. Repetition can prioritize an issue; it does not prove the objection is valid or that the whole market agrees. Preserve mandate differences and sampling limits. Rerun only affected tests, plus cross-module checks for changed assumptions.

Deliver the top concrete preparation actions and proposed owners, an improved answer grounded in existing evidence, and the open questions needing new records. Draft follow-up communications only when requested; sending remains separately authorized.
