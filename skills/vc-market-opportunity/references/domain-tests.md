# Market opportunity

Question: can reachable buyer demand support the growth and return case? Use [the shared test contract](review-method.md) and the [market and investor definitions](#market-and-investor-definitions).

Inputs: segment-level account or transaction counts, source dates/geographies, realistic realized prices, budget ownership, qualification exclusions, adoption evidence, expansion assumptions and investor economics where known.

## High-value tests

1. **Rebuild demand from eligible buyers.** Recalculate buyer counts × plausible spend or qualifying transactions × take rate. Deduplicate groups, subsidiaries, and overlapping segments. Challenge each eligibility filter with actual customer characteristics. The falsifier is not a smaller top-down report; it is evidence that material counted buyers cannot buy, lack the budget, or receive insufficient value. Produce a sourced total/serviceable/obtainable bridge with uncertain inputs explicit.
2. **Willingness to pay versus economic value.** Ask which budget pays and what can actually be captured after the customer retains its benefit. Compare representative contracts or paid tests to forecast price. Labor savings, existing software budgets, and transaction value cannot all be treated as additive revenue pools without a coherent buying model.
3. **Structural obstacles and slower adoption.** Ask why this problem persists and whether fragmentation, integration, local regulation, procurement or low frequency limits capture. Model the company-specified or illustrative adoption slowdown and a narrower initial segment; update selling/deployment limits. A large theoretical TAM cannot rebut observed lack of reachable demand.
4. **Venture-return feasibility.** Hand the obtainable revenue path and pricing to the financial module. Compare the exit outcomes required by the actual mandate with reachable scenarios; do not assume every deal must return a whole fund or that a check size alone determines the required exit. Emerging demand belongs in the category/timing module as a tested hypothesis.

Output: auditable market build, segment exclusions, adoption sensitivities, strongest demand evidence, and the next discriminating buyer test. Update the market slide and model's penetration assumptions together.

Handoffs: price/budget → business model; achievable buyer capture → scalability and finance; unmet demand/category uncertainty → category/timing; substitutes and market boundaries → competition.

## Market and investor definitions


- Build a bottom-up market: eligible paying entities or transaction volume × realistic spend/take rate, with sources for each term. Separate total market, serviceable market, and a time-bounded obtainable share.
- Reconcile labor spend, software spend, transaction value, and the revenue the company can capture. Avoid counting both displaced labor and existing software as additive demand when they fund the same workflow.
- Filter by geography, industry, customer size, data readiness, procurement constraints, channel access, and adoption timing. Use observed ACVs for comparable customers; do not multiply a narrow premium ACV across all entities without support.
- Test expansion markets as hypotheses with requirements, cost, and proof points. A broad adjacent budget category is not evidence of cross-sell ability.
- Compare direct rivals, incumbent bundles, model providers, internal builds, service providers, and doing nothing on buyer-relevant dimensions. Verify current capabilities and prices from primary sources; identify source marketing claims as such.
- Explain why now, the wedge, the path to durable advantage, and the competitor or substitute most likely to win. Include win/loss evidence and falsifiable differentiation rather than unsupported superlatives.
- If valuation/terms are supplied, show an illustrative return bridge: entry ownership, future dilution, exit equity value, preference/debt effects when material, and expected holding period. Derive outcome requirements from the investor's supplied hurdle; do not invent a universal venture threshold or promise returns.
- Connect proceeds to measurable milestones, hiring/capacity assumptions, runway cushion, and financing dependence. Ask what evidence supports the next round if capital is harder to obtain. Without terms, mark return feasibility unassessed while still reviewing the business.
