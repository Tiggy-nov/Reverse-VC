# Fundraising amount, use of capital and proof

Use when the requested raise, use of proceeds, timing or next milestone changes the domain conclusion, or when a fundraising assessment is requested. It is a decision input across the suite, not a requirement to run a full financing model for every narrow test.

## Establish what the number means

Keep distinct: proposed current primary raise; any secondary sale; target/minimum/stretch scenarios; legally documented secured financing versus pipeline/interest; cash already received; historical funds raised; proposed valuation; and one investor's check. Record currency, timing, instrument, fees, obligations and restrictions where supplied. Secondary proceeds do not fund company operations. A verbal commitment is not cash.

Determine whether the plan's opening cash already contains earlier financing, to avoid adding it twice. Use actual timing for cash before close; a round that closes after the cash breach cannot solve that breach without an explicit bridge. Apply restrictions and transaction/debt payments to the appropriate cash schedule without subtracting them again if already included in the operating forecast.

Unknown terms, timing or cost allocations remain unknown. Do not reverse-engineer a valuation, dilution, normal round size or whole-fund return requirement from the requested amount. SAFEs, notes, multiple share classes, preferences and follow-ons need their actual mechanics before ownership/return calculations.

## Bridge capital to evidence

Map the material uses of capital to a milestone:

Current evidence → uncertainty or operating constraint → work to resolve it → owner/capability → cost and payment timing → milestone and acceptance criterion → resulting operating/financing decision.

Reconcile the use-of-proceeds plan with hiring/ramp, product work, compute/data, sales/marketing, deployment/support, working capital and relevant corporate costs. Work may serve multiple milestones; allocate or flag overlaps rather than add the same payroll or engineering expense twice. More funding cannot accelerate a procurement, evaluation, certification or hiring timeline without an evidenced mechanism.

Keep three conclusions separate:

1. **Business/domain evidence:** what is true or unresolved today.
2. **Capital-plan feasibility:** whether accessible cash can fund the scoped milestones with timing and downside understood.
3. **Fundraise/mandate fit:** whether the stated proof, plan and terms fit the particular investor's requirements, where known.

A larger ask increases the amount and scope of spending to explain; it does not invent mature cohorts or change the company's actual stage. A smaller ask may reduce the operating scope or leave an external dependency, without making the customer problem less real. Make any additional evidence demand proportional to the proposed plan, irreversible commitments and risk.

## When cash calculations are possible

Use dated cash movements rather than an arbitrary months-of-runway rule:

Closing cash = opening available cash + collections + primary financing received + other specified inflows - operating cash outflows - capex/investment - financing/debt payments and other specified outflows.

Each movement appears once. Funding fees may reduce financing proceeds or appear as an outflow, not both. Revenue is not automatically collections; commitments may require prepayment, milestone payments or minimum usage. Name minimum cash, expected breach/financing deadlines and any chosen buffer. Monthly totals may conceal an earlier intramonth need.

When the records cannot support cash arithmetic, identify the missing cost/timing inputs and limit the conclusion. Do not create precision from a nominal burn rate when the planned expense profile changes.

## Compare amounts dynamically

Use the supplied minimum/base/expanded round cases or derive candidate cases from itemized milestone costs with assumptions explicit. No default dollar brackets, universal Seed/A raise limits, ARR floors or fixed runway target.

Hold current company facts and the existing-plan baseline fixed. Then show what a different amount actually changes: milestones attempted, spending, hires/ramp, eligible customers served, workload capacity, proof window and financing dependency. Distinguish more cash for the same plan from a genuinely expanded plan.

Test delayed/partial financing or a slower milestone when material. Explain underfunding, concentration of capital in an unproved assumption, or premature scope expansion through the specific plan. A stretch budget is not automatically wasteful, nor a lean plan automatically efficient. Recommend a revised scope or sequence only where evidence/costs support it.

## How the raise changes each specialist

| Specialist | Relevant capital question |
| --- | --- |
| Customer problem | Which customer/segment hypotheses must be resolved before the planned spend; can the proposed discovery produce that evidence? A larger raise does not validate the problem. |
| Venture scale | Which links from wedge to expansion the round can establish, capital intensity/financing dependence, and term-based investor fit where known |
| Product/technology | Which quality, reliability, data-access or technical milestones are funded; actual dependencies and evidence thresholds |
| Market | Whether obtainable adoption, eligible segments and expansion timing are supported by the financed reach/deployment plan |
| Competition | Whether the proposed spend can build or defend the claimed advantage, and what can be copied or bundled before it does |
| Business model | Whether the capital tests unproven pricing/cost drivers or simply finances greater losses; subsidy/credit expiry and delivery economics |
| GTM | Which segment, channel, pilot/production motion and qualified capacity the budget funds, and when retained economics become observable |
| Scalability | Which binding constraints funded hiring, automation or capacity can relieve, with realistic lead time and cash impact |
| Financial model | Whether uses, scenarios, cash timing, milestone deadlines and any investor-return calculations reconcile |
| Category/timing | Whether the company can fund buyer education and survive adoption delay; which demand/timing proof precedes expansion |
| Team/governance | Which capabilities are funded and available when needed; recruitment/ramp, interim responsibility and oversight of commitments |
| Coordinator | Reconcile all linked plans and eliminate duplicate cost/proof claims; retain separate business, capital and mandate conclusions |

## Compact output

For a relevant domain review include: requested raise and basis; milestone it is meant to buy; capital/timing assumptions affecting this test; sufficiency or unknowns; smaller/larger/delayed scenario if decision-useful; and next proof/action. Preserve a separate domain conclusion if amount or terms are absent. Reuse a prior verified cash/raise analysis and identify any changed assumption instead of recomputing inconsistent private versions.
