# GTM experiments, scenarios and rehearsal

Use supplied evidence before proposing new research. Choose the smallest test that can distinguish the commercial explanations. The examples below are options; they are not a required checklist or universal proof threshold.

## Test contract

For each priority test: hypothesis and alternative; source claim; eligible customer population; procedure and comparison; measure and observation window; success/failure/inconclusive criterion and basis; proposed owner; time, cost and capacity; stop/continue decision; dependencies. Separate executed record checks from experiments that have not run.

Prefer criteria from customer commitments, acceptable economics, actual cash constraints or a justified learning objective. Define them before interpreting new results. Small samples may improve understanding without estimating population conversion. If a randomized comparison is infeasible, name the alternative design and its causal limits; do not promise statistical significance without a sample/variance basis.

## Representative tests

| Uncertainty | Useful discriminating test | Decision and limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Right ICP or simply friendly access? | Compare behavior and paid commitments from independently recruited, qualified prospects with founder-network prospects; record segment differences. | Whether demand transfers. A warm relationship is not automatically invalid, and groups may differ. |
| Weak need or unclear message? | Unprompted buyer reconstruction and a transparent offer to comparable eligible prospects; track downstream commitments. | Whether clarity changes behavior. Message preference alone cannot validate demand. |
| Need reference proof or immediate workflow value? | Compare the buyer's requested proof and actual evaluation steps; test a credible reference or representative workload demonstration. | Which selling work matters. Do not infer from industry stereotype alone. |
| Pilot success or production purchase? | Agree outcome criteria, buyer, budget path and a decision date; record all outcomes and pending cases. | Whether a replicable production offer exists. Do not assume automatic conversion or charge without agreement. |
| Activation friction or poor ongoing value? | Observe first value and repeat value separately; test one documented onboarding obstacle for comparable cohorts. | Whether adoption or delivered value is limiting. Preserve the cost of assistance. |
| Incremental acquisition or attribution overlap? | Where feasible and authorized, use a holdout, staged geography/cohort rollout or other justified comparison and deduplicate identities. | Whether the channel adds retained outcomes. Spillovers and selection may limit causality. |
| Founder dependence or a new seller's ramp? | Compare matched accounts and cohorts, with founder intervention logged, over a meaningful selling window. | Which steps can transfer. Different segment/age cannot be attributed solely to seller skill. |
| AI-assisted GTM productivity? | Compare qualified accepted opportunities and later paid outcomes per total dollar/hour, including QA and failed attempts. | Whether automation improves the business. Output volume, open rates and meeting counts alone are insufficient. |
| Durable channel or saturation? | A bounded spend/reach increase with marginal CAC and cohort quality observed. | Whether to increase budget. Do not assume linear response beyond the tested range. |
| AI-search visibility or qualified demand? | Repeated relevant intent queries plus observed referral/CRM/self-report evidence and downstream activation. | Whether discovery contributes. Mention share cannot establish incremental sales. |

Execution may involve personal data, live budgets or customer communications. The review can draft a plan and materials; it must not launch campaigns, contact customers, enroll subjects, spend money or change live systems without applicable authorization.

## Coherent stress scenarios

Select the scenario implied by the company's material risk; use company-supplied values where possible, otherwise label illustrative shocks:

- Founder unavailable: adjust which selling/evaluation steps stop, pipeline progression, support, seller cost and cash timing; don't assume every account disappears.
- Largest acquisition partner/channel weakened: change eligible reach, lead quality, fees, conversion, channel replacement time and customer ownership constraints.
- Pilot buying committee delays production: keep affected pilots pending, delay deployment/revenue/collection and include continuing implementation effort.
- Broad models or incumbent bundles improve: state what customer job might become cheaper/easier elsewhere; change win rate, price or retention only as explicit assumptions, with verification needs.
- Spend doubles: model marginal audience quality, conversion and workload plus ramp and delivery constraints; avoid doubling all downstream results by default.
- Paid acquisition grows while cohorts weaken: show retained customers and gross profit by channel/cohort, not only aggregate new revenue.
- Enterprise adoption slows: connect security/data approval, integration and training delays to time to value, graduation and financing needs.

Show baseline, changed assumptions, calculated outcome, break point and possible response. A mitigation remains a proposal until executed and verified. If outputs improve only by optimistic untested assumptions, say which evidence is missing.

## Live founder rehearsal

Start with the most consequential supported objection or missing proof, grounded in a source. Ask one concrete question, then wait. Useful starting questions include:

- Which customer outside your immediate network bought for the same reason as your best early customer?
- What happened to every pilot whose agreed decision date has passed?
- Which acquisition channel has produced retained paid customers after accounting for all the selling and delivery effort?
- Where does the customer's path stop before they obtain useful value?

Choose one that fits; do not dump the list or invent responses. Treat confidence and hesitation as presentation observations rather than evidence. If a record is unavailable, log the gap and next useful proof rather than press indefinitely.

## Prioritization and next plan

Rank by decision impact, uncertainty, time/cost to learn and dependency. A 30/60/90-day roadmap can be useful when requested, but an enterprise buying cycle may require a longer result window. Do not force evidence to arrive on an arbitrary calendar. Keep baseline evidence fixed and record whether new results change a claim, economics, audience or merely wording.
