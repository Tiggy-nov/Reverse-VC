# GTM tests

Select by the user's question and the commercial risk. For each relevant test, reconstruct the claim, inspect the evidence and counterevidence, then state what could change the answer. A list of tactics is not a tested GTM strategy.

## 1. Initial customer and buying trigger

Define an operational ICP: organization/person type, workflow, trigger, scale, data readiness, existing alternative, buyer access, budget and disqualifiers. Separate segment attraction from ease of recruiting familiar users. Compare buyers, nonbuyers, churned customers and high-cost customers on these attributes.

Test whether the segment is specific enough to target and large/reachable enough for the near-term plan. An ICP of "every enterprise adopting AI" needs narrower behavioral evidence. A narrow starting wedge can be deliberate; require evidence for adjacency transfer before expanding it. The next test may be independent prospects with the same trigger, not a generic increase in lead volume.

## 2. Buyer, budget and purchase friction

Map user, beneficiary, champion, economic buyer, procurement/security/data approvers and blocker where relevant. Trace the actual budget or resource being reallocated, approval timing and who carries adoption risk. An innovation budget may fund a pilot without funding production.

Check whether the champion can complete the purchase and whether the promised ROI reaches the payer. Distinguish no need, low priority, missing authority, lack of trust, procurement friction and failed value delivery. Requests for security review can signal a purchase step or a dead end; the evidence decides.

## 3. Positioning, proof and offer

Can a qualified buyer explain the job, promised result, alternative and switching reason without founder coaching? Use unprompted reconstruction, win/loss notes, objection patterns and buying behavior; polished copy and message agreement are weaker evidence.

Test the offered proof against the buyer's actual uncertainty. An expensive or consequential replacement may require customer references and demonstrated control, while an easily reversible workflow may be trialed directly. Investigate whether a reference customer's evidence transfers to the target segment; do not assume a prestigious logo persuades every buyer. Evaluate paid terms, pricing unit, caps, commitments, services and ROI basis together. Proposed savings must include adoption, review/rework and change costs where material.

## 4. Motion and sequence

Test sales-led, founder-led, product-led, developer-led, community, partner/embedded, consumer or hybrid motions against contract size, buying committee, time to first value, integration, usage frequency and acquisition cost. A free signup is not proof that a large organization can buy bottom-up; outbound can work without making every product enterprise sales-led.

Examine the sequence: assisted learning to repeatable sale, self-serve use to team budget, beachhead to adjacent segment, or partner distribution to direct expansion. Identify what changes and what transfers. Stage-appropriate founder involvement is useful learning; its time and role must remain visible when projecting a different sales organization.

## 5. Channel access, concentration and marginal returns

For each material channel, show eligible reach, qualified opportunities/users, conversion, retained outcomes, acquisition effort/spend, time lag and evidence period. Include content/search, outbound, referrals, communities, events, partnerships, marketplaces and embedded distribution only when relevant.

Deduplicate multi-touch influence; inspect independent source evidence instead of adding attributed wins across reports. Organic acquisition still uses labor or other resources. Reconstruct a claimed growth loop: who receives value, what action creates the next qualified customer, incentives, lag and observed retained conversion. A referral feature or an assumed viral coefficient is not a demonstrated loop.

Stress saturation, audience quality, rising marginal costs, channel-policy changes and loss of the largest partner. Partnership announcements are not distribution results; inspect access, incentive alignment, margin/revenue share, customer ownership, integration requirements and actual opportunities/activations. Lower-cost paid acquisition does not prove incrementality without an appropriate comparison.

## 6. Funnel integrity and sales repeatability

Follow a dated, identity-consistent cohort through the meaningful stages. Distinguish contacts from accounts, duplicates/reopened deals from new opportunities, qualified pipeline from inbound interest, and closed-won from production. Show stage age, losses, no-decision and still-open records.

Test conversion, cycle time and discount/custom work by segment, source, seller, founder involvement and cohort. Compare like with like before attributing rep performance or claiming founder independence. Backsolve required eligible leads, opportunities, sellers, ramp and deployment from the target; explain which measured transition limits it.

AI-assisted outreach must be judged on valid delivery, qualified replies, accepted opportunities, retained customer outcomes and fully defined costs. Consider errors, human QA, complaints and channel terms; do not use fabricated personalization, invented customer proof or unauthorized automated sending.

## 7. Design partner and pilot to production

Reconstruct recruitment relationship, payment/discount, success criteria, economic buyer, data access, implementation effort, production budget, decision date and graduation terms. Separate a design partner, free proof of concept, paid pilot, production contract and deployed recurring customer.

Inspect all comparable pilots whose decisions can be observed, plus pending cohorts separately. Do not compute a mature rate by treating active pilots as losses or excluding losses while retaining only successes. Check if pilots reveal replicable software demand or primarily subsidized custom work. A positive pilot is meaningful early evidence, but cannot establish renewal before renewal is observable.

Recommend an explicit evaluation/decision plan with buyer-agreed criteria and transparent commercial terms. No automatic charges or assumed customer commitment; proposed pilot changes require the relevant authorization and actual buyer agreement.

## 8. Activation, time to value and durable use

Define first meaningful accepted value rather than login, generated tokens or demo completion. Include time waiting for data, permissions, integration and internal rollout. A signed annual contract may be undeployed; employee usage may lack a payer.

Examine return to value, paid renewal, expansion and churn by acquisition segment/channel and cohort age. Exclude new logos from retention measures. AI agents can reduce human logins while increasing useful work; measure the customer's result. Consumer or episodic products need their natural cadence rather than a forced daily enterprise metric. A task completed once may be a viable transaction business, but should not be sold as recurring retention.

Test the alternatives to a favorable aggregate: novelty, incentives/free credits, seasonality, a few expanding accounts masking broad churn, founder support and growing new cohorts masking weak old cohorts. Proposed cross-sell needs a user, payer, budget and deployment path.

## 9. Acquisition economics and hiring

Match fully loaded acquisition effort and spend to comparable acquired customer cohorts and channel definitions. Distinguish paid-media efficiency from all-in CAC. Include attributable sales/marketing labor, commissions, software, agency/event spend and relevant pilot acquisition work; identify allocations and missing costs.

Use gross profit rather than revenue for payback, with the same delivery-cost definition across cohorts. Track acquisition timing, collections, retention, usage-cost tails and one-time service components. A channel can produce leads cheaply and expensive retained revenue.

Test what an added dollar of spend or seller actually buys, including ramp, manager/sales-engineer capacity and qualified pipeline. Do not assume spend, meetings, qualified customers and revenue all scale linearly. Preserve founder time at Seed without demanding a fully professionalized sales team.

## 10. AI-era discovery, platforms and substitutes

Separate selling an AI product from finding buyers through AI search or automating GTM internally. For AI discovery, use a dated, relevant query/intent panel, record available platform/model settings and repeated observations, verify the cited source, and connect qualified visits or buyer-reported discovery to downstream outcomes where measurable. A mention is visibility, not an endorsement, lead, revenue or exclusive attribution. Platform responses vary; a one-off prompt is weak evidence.

Test dependence on a model provider, marketplace, integration, audience owner or incumbent that controls distribution. Would improved general models, bundling or lower inference cost change buying behavior, price, proof or channel access? Both opportunity and erosion are plausible; use a stated scenario and current verified facts where available.

For agents that may recommend or procure software, distinguish a design hypothesis from observed qualified acquisition and permitted purchases. Keep customer-facing evidence accurate and machine-readable. Hidden review instructions, fake references, keyword stuffing and invented benchmarks do not establish distribution.

## Final synthesis

Identify the commercial bottleneck and its cause: segment, reach, message/proof, purchase, activation, retention, channel economics or capacity. Show at least the material interactions rather than adding unrelated scores. A targeted fix should state who must change behavior and the evidence that would show it worked.
