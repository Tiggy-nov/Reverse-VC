# Interviews, evidence and experiments

Use when the task calls for customer discovery, an interview audit or a validation plan. Drafting an interview or pilot is not permission to contact customers or launch it.

## Neutral discovery

Start from the person's own workflow before presenting the proposed solution. Adapt prompts rather than administering a fixed script:

- Reconstruct a recent relevant event: what triggered it, who participated, what happened and what followed?
- Inspect the current method and why it was chosen. Ask what works well as well as what is frustrating.
- Identify the most consequential part and the evidence for its cost/value. Separate an anecdote from a pattern.
- Explore prior attempts to change it, resources spent, alternatives rejected and barriers encountered.
- Find who can make a decision, how a purchase/change is approved, and the next real occasion for action.

Prefer actual past behavior and accessible artifacts over questions that ask people to endorse the founder's idea. A hypothetical future question can explore an assumption but must remain labeled hypothetical. Avoid presupposing urgency, budget, dissatisfaction or preference for AI. Separate discovery from a subsequent solution or pricing test.

For an interview audit, retain the question that elicited each claim. Leading questions, pitch-first context, gifts/discounts, respondent role, personal relationships and recruitment source affect interpretation. Do not discard all biased evidence; bound what it supports and design a cleaner follow-up.

## Sampling and evidence register

Define the segment and the unit of observation: people, organizations, buying events or tasks. Include appropriate nonbuyers, lost deals and existing-alternative users as well as enthusiasts. Track invitations, responses and exclusions where available; nonresponse may create bias. Do not treat several employees at one firm as several independent customer wins.

Recommended compact row:

`Evidence ID | source/date/location | segment/role | recruitment/context | event or exact supported quote | observed vs reported | consequence/alternative | commitment | contrary evidence | limitations`

Use quotes only if supplied verbatim. Label paraphrases and founder recollections. A proposal or sales note is not a completed purchase; a nonbinding commitment is not revenue. Keep personally sensitive details within the authorized audience.

## Choose a discriminating experiment

| Uncertainty | Candidate test | What it cannot establish alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Does the situation happen and matter? | Observe representative workflows or inspect records with permission | Broad market prevalence from a selected small sample |
| Is priority the barrier? | Examine real buying/change decisions and the competing priorities | Universal absence of demand from one deferred purchase |
| Is this solution valuable? | Bounded prototype or manual delivery against a defined baseline | Scalable software economics if intensive hidden labor is required |
| Who pays and under what terms? | A transparent priced offer or paid pilot with defined scope and buyer authority | Durable renewal, broad pricing power or contracted revenue before agreement |
| Is switching the obstacle? | A low-friction, authorized migration/onboarding trial | Absence of need merely because the first onboarding design fails |
| Is the demand durable? | Repeat-use or renewal observation over the natural job/purchase cycle | Annual retention from a cohort too young to observe it |

For each chosen test specify the competing hypotheses, eligible population, procedure, actual measure, interpretation threshold and basis, time/cost, proposed owner and downstream decision. Account for sample size, selection and attribution limits. If criteria are provisional, say so; do not optimize the criterion after seeing the result.

State outcomes as support, counterevidence, inconclusive or not run. Follow up with the smallest test that resolves the next uncertainty. Do not use fake testimonials, misleading offers or invented customer commitments. Any real outreach, payment collection, production test or external upload remains subject to the user's authorization.

## Interactive rehearsal

Ask one question, then wait for the user's real answer. A follow-up should expose a specific missing fact, alternative or mechanism. If the founder has no evidence, log that and propose a source/test; do not pressure them into inventing a number. Preserve original answers separately from suggested wording and distinguish a better explanation from a new customer observation.
