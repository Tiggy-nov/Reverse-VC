# Customer Problem Stress Test

Answer: whose problem or unmet desire is this, what makes it matter, what do people actually do about it, and what evidence would change the company's understanding? A convincing description of pain is not proof that customers will adopt this solution or support a venture-scale business.

## Company and fundraising lens

Default to AI-native B2B and enterprise businesses at Seed or Series A. Read [company-calibration.md](references/company-calibration.md) first, or reuse an established source-backed context: product/workflow, market and buyer, business model, actual maturity and proposed current raise. Apply only relevant tests and follow an explicit user scope override.

When the fundraising amount, timing or use of proceeds changes this review, read [raise-calibration.md](references/raise-calibration.md). Connect capital to the specific proof and operating milestones it buys; distinguish requested primary capital, secondary, secured funds and existing cash. If the amount is missing, continue the domain work and request it only when needed for the capital conclusion. A larger raise does not itself establish maturity, and a narrow test does not require a full financing audit.

State the applied business/stage lens and any material effect of the raise on the result. Keep current evidence, capital-plan feasibility and investor fit distinct; share definitions and source IDs across reviews.

## Start with the customer situation

From supplied material, reconstruct: affected person/organization, context and trigger, job or desired outcome, current alternative, consequence of the status quo, frequency or exposure, decision maker, budget/resource owner, and evidence period. Keep user, beneficiary, champion, buyer and approver distinct when they differ.

Write a problem hypothesis independent of the proposed product: **when [situation], [person/organization] struggles to achieve [outcome], causing [observed consequence], and currently responds by [behavior].** For opportunity-led B2B products, or consumer products when explicitly requested, describe the desired outcome and existing behavior without manufacturing business pain or requiring an enterprise budget.

If only a pitch is supplied, identify claims to validate. If interviews, logs or contracts exist, inspect them before asking the user to repeat information. No materials means a concise intake or interview plan, not a validated problem verdict. Default to analyzing the evidence; use live one-question-at-a-time interrogation only when requested. Never invent customer replies, quotes, interviews or willingness to pay.

## Examine the problem and its alternatives

Read [problem-tests.md](references/problem-tests.md). Test the causal problem, customer consequences, priority, workarounds, payer/budget path, adoption friction, and segment consistency. Seek disconfirming cases as well as the strongest evidence of demand.

Use a neutral stance: the problem may be real, latent, rare but consequential, poorly framed, segment-specific, already solved sufficiently, or unsupported. Low frequency does not imply low value; loud complaints do not necessarily imply priority. Lack of current spend can reflect a genuine unmet need, a barrier, or lack of value; investigate rather than assume.

For AI-native pitches, separate **customer job → needed outcome → proposed method**. Ask whether AI changes feasibility/value enough to alter customer behavior, and whether model error, sensitive data, trust, review or integration creates a new burden. A desire to adopt AI does not by itself establish a customer problem. A simpler existing method may already suffice; compare actual alternatives rather than presume either conclusion.

## Audit the evidence, not just the story

Keep a segment-level evidence table with source ID/location, role, sampling/recruitment, date, reported statement, observed behavior, consequence, commitment, conflicting evidence and limitations. Distinguish direct observation, customer recollection, management interpretation and a reviewer hypothesis. Preserve separate organizations and repeated interviews to avoid double-counting.

Interpret signals in context. Compliments, survey intent, waitlists and nonbinding letters can guide discovery but do not equal purchase or durable use. Paid pilots can support willingness to try under their actual terms; assess discounts, relationships, budgets, effort and subsequent usage. Declined or abandoned purchases need reasons; they may expose sales, security or timing friction rather than prove no problem exists.

For small or selected samples, state what the evidence covers. Do not generalize interviews with founder friends, current enthusiasts or one job role to an entire market. Do not impose a universal number of interviews or pass rate. For a novel category, specify the behavior that would demonstrate emerging demand rather than demand an already-established category.

## Produce the next discriminating test

Read [interviews-and-experiments.md](references/interviews-and-experiments.md) when designing discovery or interpreting interview/experiment evidence. State the competing hypotheses, eligible participants, sampling plan, procedure, measure, pre-specified success/failure interpretation and why it fits, plus cost/time and proposed owner where known. Label proposed criteria and unexecuted work clearly.

Prioritize tests that distinguish explanations: weak underlying need versus poorly chosen customer; lack of priority versus purchase friction; no budget versus wrong payer; solution failure versus problem absence. Use existing records before requiring new research. Do not automate contacting people, charging them, making promises or deploying an experiment without authorization.

In live founder mode, ask one concrete question and wait for the actual answer. Follow up on a missing example, source, consequence or alternative. If evidence is unavailable, log the gap and next step. Keep the founder's answer separate from suggested wording. A simulated customer persona can help prepare questions but cannot validate demand.

## Deliver a useful result

Lead with a scoped conclusion: **well-evidenced in the named segment; promising but unproven; meaningful but low current priority or blocked adoption; misframed problem/segment; evidence against the stated hypothesis; or insufficient evidence.** Several can apply to different segments. Give confidence and source coverage without converting it into a funding probability.

Provide the best-supported problem statement, contrary evidence and alternatives, buyer/beneficiary map, remaining uncertainties, and a small prioritized validation plan. Distinguish problem existence, severity/value, priority/commitment and solution adoption in the conclusion. Suggest more precise pitch wording only when requested or necessary to show how the evidence changes the claim; preserve missing proof.

Do not automatically conclude that a strong problem implies PMF, defensibility, a large market or a viable business model. For a broader review, hand off segment, observed consequences, current alternatives, willingness-to-pay/commitment evidence, adoption barriers, claim IDs and test status. A venture-scale assessment then evaluates the size and economics of the opportunity.

This skill works independently; when Reverse VC Scaling is present, reuse the same context/evidence IDs and route findings to its product, market, business-model, category and scalability modules. Stop when further certainty needs real customer evidence. [research-notes.md](references/research-notes.md) records conceptual grounding and its limits.
