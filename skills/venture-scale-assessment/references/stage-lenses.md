# Seed and Series A evidence lenses

These are working analytical lenses, not universal VC policies. Default to AI-native B2B/enterprise at Seed or Series A and use [company-calibration.md](company-calibration.md). Adjust to the actual fund, geography, business model and technical/commercial maturity. Capital round names, calendar age and revenue amounts cannot by themselves prove product-market fit.

| Decision dimension | Seed lens | Series A lens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer problem | Specific observed pain, desire or constraint; credible buyer/user; evidence from behavior as available | Repeated evidence in a defined segment, including why buyers act and why some do not |
| Product or technical thesis | Prototype, enabling experiment, differentiated insight, early outcome; explicit limits | Repeatable delivered value or a material technical/regulatory milestone appropriate to the sector |
| Demand and durability | Early pull, committed access/time, paid discovery or pilot, usage where meaningful; distinguish curiosity from commitment | Conversion, retained use/renewal/expansion where cohorts allow, customer outcomes, concentration and loss analysis; no invented mature history |
| Distribution | Plausible reachable wedge and a test of access; founder-led acquisition may be appropriate | Emerging repeatable motion with conversion, cycle and deployment evidence; identify remaining founder dependence |
| Economics | Plausible value capture and a testable cost model; explicit unproven drivers | Measured economics for relevant segments/workloads with a credible improvement plan; avoid blended metrics concealing problem cohorts |
| Market and expansion | A believable path from the first segment to a valuable opportunity; prove the first enabling links | Initial penetration, adjacent demand evidence and capacity to pursue expansion without assuming every adjacency |
| Team | Relevant insight, ability to learn and build, access/advantages, critical gaps | Execution through milestones, ability to recruit/delegate, leadership gaps tied to the next operating plan |
| Capital | The next falsifiable proof the round buys, including failure/learning branches | Costed milestones, cash sensitivity, ramp and financing dependence; do not assume the next round arrives on schedule |

## Interpret evidence at the correct maturity

- Short-lived cohorts cannot establish annual retention or lifetime value. Report censoring and observe the available repeat-use window.
- One large customer can validate value and create concentration risk at the same time. Founder-connected pilots are useful evidence with selection limitations, not automatically worthless or representative.
- High revenue can coexist with bespoke services or subsidized growth. Low revenue can coexist with meaningful technical de-risking in long-development-cycle businesses.
- Founder-led selling at Series A can be appropriate. The question is what has been learned about a repeatable motion and the remaining transfer risk, not whether all founder involvement has vanished.
- A pre-revenue Series A in deep technology needs a context-specific technical, regulatory, manufacturing and financing case. Do not waive scrutiny; change the milestone being tested.

## Business-model adaptations

| Business type | Evidence that may be relevant | Common false inference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Enterprise / vertical AI | Production workflows, paid conversion, time to value, cohort renewal when observable, implementation burden, data rights and human effort | Paid pilots or contracted ceilings are automatically durable ARR |
| SMB/midmarket B2B self-serve | Business-account activation and paid retention, buyer access, channel quality, onboarding burden and cost-to-serve | User signups equal paying businesses or every B2B product needs long enterprise selling |
| Marketplace | Liquidity, repeat transactions on each side, contribution after incentives, net revenue and geographic density | GMV equals revenue or a subsidized spike establishes scalable liquidity |
| Usage infrastructure | Sustained production workloads, paid expansion, concentration, capacity and contribution per relevant unit | Increased volume guarantees improved net revenue or economics |
| Deep tech / regulated | Independently reproducible technical results, feasibility boundaries, permissions path, manufacturing/compute needs and conditional customer commitments | No ARR means no investment case, or a lab result removes commercialization risk |
| Emerging category | A clear prospective beneficiary, enabling change, early behavioral commitment, adoption/education experiments and funding path | No existing budget proves no opportunity, or a founder-defined category proves a large market |

## Round-to-milestone bridge

For each material uncertainty, connect current proof → next test → cost/time → pass/fail or learning result → resulting financing/operating decision. Use source-backed timing and costs where available. A proposed target is not achieved traction; a funding event is not a product-market-fit test. Recognize uncorrelated risks and dependencies rather than assuming one successful experiment resolves all of them.

Use [raise-calibration.md](raise-calibration.md) to compare the actual proposed amounts and net company cash, timing, uses and milestones. A large Seed raise can fund capital-intensive proof without becoming Series A by size; judge the financed plan and preserve the unproven commercial risk.

If the user asks how a VC would evaluate the same company at both Seed and Series A, hold company facts fixed and explicitly show which conclusions change with the proof standard. Do not invent an older or more mature version of the company to force a difference.
