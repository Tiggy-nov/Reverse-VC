# Company, market and stage calibration

Default scope: AI-native B2B and enterprise businesses at Seed or Series A. Follow explicit user/company context if it calls for a different scope; do not silently substitute consumer acquisition or mature growth-stage benchmarks. This reference is bundled in each specialist so no coordinator installation is required.

## Build or reuse a compact review context

Use sources already available; reuse the same context and definitions across skills. Capture only fields material to the requested test, distinguishing known facts, management assertions, assumptions and unknowns:

- Product/job and AI role: application, agent, service, developer tool, infrastructure or model; what result the customer accepts; own versus third-party technology and human work.
- Target market: vertical/horizontal, geography, eligible account size/segment, established budget or emerging demand, current alternatives, buying trigger and consequences of failure.
- Commercial system: user, champion, buyer, budget and approvers; customer access; sales/procurement cycle; deployment/data/integration needs; repeat-use cadence; price and revenue/cost model.
- Actual maturity: stated Seed/Series A round, prototype/pilot/production status, source periods, cohort ages, paying/deployed customers, revenue definitions, what is measured and what is planned.
- Fundraising context when relevant: current proposed round and currency, primary versus secondary, target/minimum/secured amounts, close timing, cash/burn, use of proceeds, milestone plan and terms if supplied. Use [raise-calibration.md](raise-calibration.md) for the analysis.

If a material field is missing, ask a concise question or show a conditional analysis while continuing useful work. Do not require a complete intake to answer one source-backed calculation. A missing fundraising amount does not block a problem, technical or market finding; it limits the capital conclusion. Do not infer the amount from the stage, valuation, total raised to date or the investor's check.

## Choose the applicable business and market lens

Models and segments can overlap; combine only relevant tests.

| Business/market situation | Prioritize | Interpretation to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SMB/midmarket AI application, including self-serve | Reachable payer, natural workflow frequency, activation-to-paid, channel quality, churn/retention maturity, low-touch onboarding and cost-to-serve | Treating every signup as an account, assuming a large-enterprise buying committee or universally short sales cycles |
| Horizontal enterprise copilot or agent | User-to-budget-owner path, differentiated workflow value, rollout/adoption, incumbent bundling, integration/permissions, procurement and production economics | Equating employee enthusiasm, a framework contract or a pilot with organization-wide deployed demand |
| Vertical or consequential enterprise workflow | Domain-specific accepted outcomes, buyer trust, severity of errors, reference transfer, data rights and deployment/approval pathway where applicable | Applying generic chatbot accuracy or presuming regulation solely from an industry label |
| Outcome-priced agent or AI-enabled service | Accepted outcome and liability allocation, retries/human work, pricing caps/disputes, delivery capacity and contribution by task/customer | Forcing seats/ARR, calling labor “automated,” or treating hours saved as realized cash savings |
| B2B developer tool, API or infrastructure | Developer versus economic buyer, production workloads, paid usage/commitments, gross-versus-net treatment, reliability, concentration, provider/capacity exposure | Calling free developer adoption recurring demand or forcing seat retention onto volatile usage |
| Foundation model, hardware or capital-intensive AI infrastructure | Reproducible technical milestones, data/compute rights, capex/contract obligations, utilization path, conditional commercialization and financing dependencies | Requiring mature SaaS ARR/NRR at Seed or treating a lab result as proof of a market |
| B2B marketplace, embedded or partner route | Paying sides/partners, liquidity and repeat transactions, net revenue/take rate, ownership of customer access, incentives and concentration | Counting GMV as revenue or a partner announcement as live distribution |
| Emerging category or new budget | Observed problem/commitment, who can authorize spending, learning/education costs, adoption sequence and competing explanations | Rejecting potential solely because no budget label exists or accepting a new category name as demand proof |

Assess substitution versus augmentation, new versus existing budget, buyer risk and deployment burden from records. Do not classify the entire company by a label when segments have different economics or buying processes. Check current primary sources when an external benchmark, rule or platform claim materially affects a live review.

## Calibrate proof to Seed versus Series A

| Dimension | Seed default | Series A default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Problem and market | Specific hypothesis with observed buyer behavior or technical/commercial access; first segment and expansion logic | Repeated value evidence in a defined segment, exclusions and measurable buying/usage patterns |
| Product and AI | Feasibility and representative early evaluation, explicit operating limits and next proof | Repeatable delivered outcomes or relevant technical/commercial milestones, known quality/cost constraints |
| GTM | Plausible access/motion, founder learning and early commitments; test transfer beyond friendly buyers | Emerging repeated conversion/activation and cohort/channel evidence; transfer and scale risks remain explicit |
| Economics | Stated value-capture/cost assumptions and a plan to measure them | Measured relevant economics and a supported improvement plan; preserve immature cohort limits |
| Capital and team | Costed route to decisive proof and the capabilities needed to run it | Costed route to the next operating/financing milestone with ramp, dependencies and downside |

Round labels, revenue levels and cash raised do not establish maturity. Series A in a long-development-cycle business may depend on reproducible technical/customer milestones rather than SaaS retention. A large Seed round may fund expensive proof while remaining Seed risk. Explain the appropriate proof and the unresolved risk rather than automatically passing or failing the company.

## Make adaptation visible

Briefly state the applied lens and the few tests or thresholds it changes: for example, accepted paid outcomes rather than seats, decision-mature pilots rather than annual renewals, or procurement plus deployment timing rather than signup conversion. Ground this choice in sources. Keep the requested domain verdict separate from round-plan feasibility and investor fit. Do not reprint the whole context in every specialist output.
