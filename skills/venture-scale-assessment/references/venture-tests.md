# Venture-scale tests and return bridge

Select the tests that can change the decision. For each: claim/source, strongest alternative explanation, actual test or proposed evidence, conditions/metric, criterion and basis, result/limitation, and next action. Criteria may come from customer requirements, investor mandate or credible unit economics; label provisional reviewer choices.

## 1. Entry point and expansion

Identify the initial paying/using segment and why the company can win there. Build an expansion ladder across customers, workflows, product lines, geographies or platform participation. For each step, specify transferable assets, new capabilities, buying/budget changes, competitive response, capital/time and evidence. Do not count several revenue pools that pay for the same workflow twice.

Test a narrow-wedge scenario and the absence/delay of the largest adjacency. What scale remains supported? Separate a measured initial market from optional expansion. Classify a possible ceiling as observed, calculated under assumptions, or unresolved. A niche can be an excellent starting point without already proving a broad platform.

## 2. Reachable economic scale

Rebuild revenue from eligible customers × realistic realized annual revenue per customer, or paid usage/transactions × net revenue per unit. Show penetration and period. Distinguish GMV, labor savings, bookings, credits, contract ceilings and actual revenue. Compare required acquisition/deployment capacity with the operating plan.

Backsolve a proposed outcome rather than assuming it: required revenue/profit → customers/usage → penetration → selling/delivery capacity → time and capital. If a revenue or earnings multiple is used, verify current relevant comparables when needed and state differences, ranges and limitations. An assumed exit multiple is a scenario, not a valuation fact. Do not install $100m ARR or a billion-dollar valuation as a universal definition of venture scale.

## 3. Durable growth and value capture

Assess repeated value, retention at observable maturities, willingness to pay, acquisition channels, gross/contribution economics and how these change with scale. Trace value to the company: customer savings do not automatically become revenue, and revenue does not automatically become durable equity value. Test margin, concentration, dilution and competitive price pressure together where linked.

For AI, examine cost/quality/latency under real workloads, human review, model/data rights, implementation effort, provider availability and pricing. Distinguish a commodity model feature from advantages in workflow, distribution or accumulated assets, while allowing evidence that model improvements benefit the business. Consider open-source or incumbent alternatives as factual comparisons, not automatic reasons to reject.

## 4. Team, timing and capital

Ask why this team can execute the first difficult steps, what learning/technical evidence supports that view, and what capability is missing. Require dates and mechanisms for material enabling changes; gradual shifts can be relevant. Test delayed adoption, technical progress and financing. Identify whether the path needs more capital than the likely economics can support, with assumptions explicit.

Record the smallest next milestone that materially changes conviction. At Seed, it may be a technical result or evidence of customer commitment. At Series A, it may be repeated deployment, retained usage, segment economics or a sector-specific de-risking result. Do not substitute a fixed milestone list for the actual business.

## 5. Investor return scenario

For simple priced equity with no intervening cash flows:

- Entry ownership = initial investment / post-money valuation.
- Exit ownership = entry ownership × retained ownership after subsequent dilution. Model follow-on purchases explicitly if present; do not treat dilution as percentage points unless stated that way.
- Gross proceeds = exit ownership × exit equity value, after relevant debt/preference/waterfall effects. Use an actual waterfall where simple pro rata ownership is misleading.
- Gross MOIC = total investor proceeds / total invested capital, including follow-ons.
- A single-inflow annualized return illustration is MOIC^(1/years) − 1; multiple cash flows require a cash-flow-timed calculation. Do not call gross performance net of fund fees/carry.
- Under the simplified pro rata case, required exit equity value = target investor proceeds / exit ownership. Whole-fund proceeds = fund size only if that specific objective is being tested.

Backsolve the business required to support this exit, then ask whether the evidence makes that path credible. Avoid circular logic that sets the exit multiple or market penetration solely to satisfy the hurdle. If check size, entry terms or hurdle is missing, state the return question cannot yet be determined and continue the business assessment.

## Concluding distinctions

Keep a matrix of long-term scale potential, stage-appropriate proof and mandate economics. A strong problem can lead to a constrained business; a potentially enormous business can have weak current evidence; a credible company can fit one fund and fail another's ownership/return requirements. State which applies and which new fact would change it. Positive and negative claims both need support.
